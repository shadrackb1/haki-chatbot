## 1. Introduction

The Cultivating Justice: Enhancing Responsible Business Conduct in Kenya's Agribusiness Sector project (hereafter "Cultivating Justice project") is a collaborative initiative dedicated to promoting responsible business conduct and operationalising the United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs) within Kenya's agribusiness sector.

The UNGPs, adopted in 2011, are the global standard for preventing and addressing business-related human rights abuses. They establish that businesses have a responsibility to respect human rights, and that states must protect against abuses.

Through a strategic partnership between the Centre for Education Policy and Climate Justice (CEPCJ), DanChurchAid and Pamoja Trust, the project bridges policy, academic innovation, and grassroots human rights defence. This Business and Human Rights Solutions Challenge leverages partner networks with Universities and Academia through the anchor institution Riara University to run an innovation design focused hackathon. CEPCJ leads the implementation by coordinating the hackathon, engaging university stakeholders and facilitating post-hackathon incubation and follow-up activities with the winning teams.

---

## 2. Background

In 2011, the United Nations (UN) unveiled the United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights. These are a set of guidelines for States and companies to prevent, address and remedy human rights abuses committed in business operations.

These principles are grounded in recognition of three foundational pillars:

1. States' existing obligations to respect, protect and fulfil human rights and fundamental freedoms.
2. The role of business enterprises as specialized organs of society performing specialized functions, required to comply with all applicable laws and to respect human rights.
3. The need for rights and obligations to be matched to appropriate and effective remedies when breached.

In 2021, following stocktaking by the UN Working Group on Business and Human Rights on ten (10) years of the implementation of the guiding principles, the working group presented the UNGPs 10+ Roadmap for the next decade. The stocktaking recognised the progress made over 10 years but acknowledged that more should be done to accelerate the adoption and entrenchment of these principles in business operations.

The Cultivating Justice Project has been championing Business and Human Rights adoption in the Kenyan Agribusiness. Implemented in partnership with DanChurchAid and Pamoja Trust, the project has worked with over 100 agribusinesses in the last twelve (12) months across Nakuru, Nyandarua and Nairobi Metropolis (Murang'a, Kajiado, Kiambu, Machakos and Nairobi) counties.

The project has sensitised these businesses on business and human rights principles and equipped them with the practical skills to incorporate these into their operations.

Hackathons are an effective tool to enhance responsible business conduct. They create an atmosphere for collaboration and provide a platform for developing innovative solutions to environmental, social and economic challenges. The "Business and Human Rights Solutions Challenge" will harness the creative power of youth in higher learning institutions to conceptualise scalable and sustainable solutions that businesses in the Kenyan agricultural sector can utilise to adopt the UNGPs 10+ Roadmap recommendations.

## 3. Collaboration between the Cultivating Justice Project and Riara University

### 3.1 About the Project

The Cultivating Justice project, a European Union-funded project implemented by DanChurchAid (DCA) in partnership with Pamoja Trust and the Centre for Education Policy and Climate Justice (CEPCJ), seeks to enhance responsible business conduct (RBC) within Kenya's agriculture sector. The project focuses on empowering civil society to document and monitor business and human rights violations in the Agribusiness sector in Kenya with the aim of promoting the rights of farmers, preventing corporate human rights violations and promoting access to remedy. The project also seeks to promote meaningful multistakeholder dialogue, reporting and experience-sharing between corporate actors, civil society and human rights organisations, governments, including EU institutions, about the implementation of human rights, social accountability and environmental due diligence.

Through Hackathons and RBC-focused curricula review, the project aims to foster the teaching and learning of business and human rights, ultimately driving youth engagement and inclusion in responsible business conduct discourse, adoption, and implementation in Kenya. Youth participants will also be positioned to spearhead innovative tools and processes that advance the promotion and application of BHR principles.

### 3.2 Suitability of Riara University as Anchor Partner

Riara University serves as an ideal anchor university for hosting the Year 2 and Year 3 hackathons due to the strategic interdisciplinary alignment of its key academic units. While the university already consists of the following academic schools: Riara Law School, School of Business, School of Computing Sciences, School of Education, School of International Relations and Diplomacy and School of Communications and Multimedia Journalism. The strategic focus is on three academic faculties with possible support from relevant expertise of the other schools:

- **School of Computing Sciences:** Provides core technical capability in software development, data management, digital prototyping, and user-centric design, ensuring student teams can build functional, secure, and scalable technology-driven tools.
- **School of Law:** Brings specialized legal expertise in human rights, environmental law, labour rights, and corporate accountability, ensuring digital innovations are solidly grounded on the UNGPs, legal frameworks and practical remedy mechanisms.
- **School of Business:** Contributes grounding in business administration, ethics, agribusiness supply chains, and social enterprise development, ensuring that prototypes are commercially viable and sustainable within real-world agricultural ecosystems.

The synergy across the three schools creates a well-rounded environment for interdisciplinary student teams to design, prototype and deploy high-impact legal-tech and business-ethics solutions tailored to Kenya's agribusiness sector.

## 4. Objectives and Expected Outcomes

### 4.1 Objectives

The Business and Human Rights Solutions Challenge will aim to meet the following objectives:

1. **Develop Technology-Driven Tools:** To create innovative digital and technological tools that enhance the capacity of civil society organizations (CSOs) and social partners to monitor, document, and report corporate human rights violations, labour rights abuses, and environmental harm in Kenya's agribusiness sector.

2. **Identify and Prototype Digital Solutions:** To prototype accessible digital solutions that lower barriers faced by aggrieved farmers and workers in obtaining information on their rights, seeking available remedies, and engaging with collective redress processes.

3. **Establish a Structured Multi-Stakeholder Platform:** To foster dialogue and structured engagement among youth, academia, civil society, the private sector, and government around the practical implementation of business and human rights (BHR) principles, generating actionable insights for the broader Cultivating Justice Project process.

### 4.2 Expected Outcomes

(a) Participation from 10 universities, each with a team of 5.

(b) Mainstreaming Business and Human Rights education in institutions of higher learning in Kenya.

(c) A winning technology solution prototype/concept to be incubated, finished and rolled out.

(d) A database of high potential technology solutions that could support the adoption of business and human rights principles in the Kenya agribusiness sector.

(e) A database of potential collaboration partners for the current and future phases of the Cultivating Justice Project, particularly the Second Hackathon to be held in 2027.

## 11. Evaluation Criteria

**Teams and participants:** Each hackathon will feature 10 teams. Each team comprises 4 student participants and a coach, evaluated under the guidance of 5 judges (Total: 55 core participants per hackathon).

The judging panel of five (5) experts will identify the best three (3) ideas during the Day 2 pitching session. The judges will assess presented solutions based on the following seven criteria:

| No. | Criterion | Weight | What Judges Assess |
|-----|-----------|--------|--------------------|
| 1 | Human Rights Alignment | 25% | Does the solution advance the UNGPs principles? Does it address a real, documented human rights violation in agribusiness? Is the rights-holders' perspective centred? |
| 2 | Impact | 25% | Potential reach (number of workers, farmers, communities affected). Ease of measuring and tracking impact. Evidence of need from target beneficiaries. |
| 3 | Innovation | 15% | Creativity and novelty of the approach. Fresh perspective on the problem. Additional value compared to existing solutions. |
| 4 | Practicality and Feasibility | 15% | How well the solution addresses current market needs. Technical feasibility. Can it be built and deployed within available resources? |
| 5 | Sustainability | 10% | Revenue potential, strategic partnerships, investment attractiveness. Viability beyond the Cultivating Justice Project support period. |
| 6 | User-Centred Design | 5% | Did the team engage affected workers/farmers during ideation? Is the solution accessible to users with low digital literacy? Does it account for local context (language, connectivity, devices)? |
| 7 | Pitch Quality | 5% | Quality of pitch deck. Professionalism of team presentation. Clarity and persuasiveness of messaging. |
| | **Total** | **100%** | |

### 11.1 Scoring Process

- Each judge scores independently on a scale of 1-10 for each criterion.
- Scores are weighted according to the percentages above.
- The highest and lowest total scores are discarded (trimmed mean) to reduce outlier bias.
- The remaining three scores are averaged to determine the final ranking.
- In the event of a tie, the Human Rights Alignment score serves as the tiebreaker.

### 11.2 Disqualification

A team may be disqualified if:

- The solution is found to plagiarise or infringe third-party intellectual property.
- The team received undisclosed external assistance beyond the permitted mentorship.
- The solution promotes activities that violate Kenyan law or international human rights standards.
- The team breached the code of conduct.

## 17. Intellectual Property Rights

### 17.1 Ownership

All intellectual property (IP) generated during the hackathon belongs to the team that created it, subject to the terms below. Each team member must sign an IP assignment agreement before participating, confirming that:

(a) The solution is their original work and does not infringe the IP rights of any third party.

(b) They assign joint ownership to all team members in equal shares.

(c) They grant the Cultivating Justice Project a non-exclusive, royalty-free, perpetual licence to use, reproduce, modify, and distribute the solution for non-commercial purposes related to the project, including reporting to the European Union, academic publication, and public demonstration.

### 17.2 Incubation IP Terms

- During the 6-month incubation period, the winning team retains ownership of all IP developed.
- The Cultivating Justice Project retains a non-exclusive licence to use the solution for project purposes.
- Any commercialisation of the solution requires a separate written agreement between the winning team and CEPCJ/Riara University.
- Third-party components (open-source libraries, datasets, APIs) remain subject to their original licences. Teams must disclose all third-party components in their submission.

### 17.3 Open-Source Option

Teams are encouraged (but not required) to release their solutions under an open-source licence (e.g., MIT, Apache 2.0) to maximise accessibility and adoption in the agribusiness sector. If a team chooses open-source, the licence must be compatible with non-commercial use and must not impose restrictions that conflict with the Cultivating Justice Project's objectives.

### 17.4 Pre-Existing IP

No participant shall use pre-existing IP (owned by the participant or a third party) in their solution without prior written consent from the IP owner. Any such usage must be disclosed in the team's submission and shall be subject to the original owner's licensing terms.

### 17.5 Waiver

Participants waive any moral rights to the extent permitted by Kenyan law, to allow the Cultivating Justice Project to use, modify, and attribute the solution as needed for project purposes.

| Performance data | Judge scores, feedback forms | Evaluation and selection | Legitimate interest (event administration) |
| Media | Photographs, video recordings, audio recordings | Documentation, reporting, promotional materials | Consent (opt-out available) |

### 18.4 Data Processing Principles

All personal data shall be processed in accordance with the principles set out in Section 25 of the Data Protection Act, 2019:

(a) **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency** -- Data is collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes. Participants are informed of the purpose at the point of collection.

(b) **Purpose limitation** -- Data is not further processed in a manner incompatible with the original purpose. Data collected for the hackathon will not be used for unrelated marketing or commercial purposes.

(c) **Data minimisation** -- Only data that is adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary is collected.

(d) **Accuracy** -- Reasonable steps are taken to ensure data is accurate and kept up to date.

(e) **Storage limitation** -- Data is retained only for as long as necessary. Participant data will be retained for 24 months after the hackathon, then securely deleted. Incubation-related data will be retained for the duration of the incubation plus 12 months.

(f) **Integrity and confidentiality** -- Appropriate technical and organisational measures are implemented to protect personal data against unauthorised access, loss, or destruction.

### 18.5 Data Security Measures

- All personal data is stored in encrypted form (AES-256 or equivalent).
- Access to participant data is restricted to authorised CEPCJ and Riara University personnel on a need-to-know basis.
- Cloud storage services used for data hosting must comply with ISO 27001 or equivalent standards.
- Physical documents containing personal data are stored in locked cabinets at CEPCJ and Riara University offices.
- Data transfers outside Kenya (e.g., to EU reporting systems) comply with Part IV of the DPA 2019 and are subject to appropriate safeguards (standard contractual clauses or equivalent).

### 18.6 Data Subject Rights

Under the Data Protection Act, 2019, participants have the right to:

- **Access** their personal data and obtain a copy (Section 26)
- **Rectification** of inaccurate data (Section 27)
- **Erasure** of data where processing is no longer necessary (Section 28)
- **Object** to processing based on legitimate interest (Section 29)
- **Lodge a complaint** with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC)

Requests should be directed to the Data Protection Officer (see Section 21).

### 18.7 Consent

- Consent for data collection is obtained at the point of registration via the registration form.
- Consent for media use (photographs, video) is obtained separately and may be withdrawn at any time by notifying the organisers in writing.
- Participants under 18 years of age must provide consent through a parent or legal guardian.
- Withdrawal of consent does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out before withdrawal.

### 18.8 Data Breach Response

In the event of a personal data breach:

1. The breach must be reported to the CEPCJ Data Protection Officer within 24 hours of discovery.
2. The Data Protection Officer will assess the severity and notify the ODPC within 72 hours if the breach is likely to result in risk to data subjects (Section 43, DPA 2019).
3. Affected data subjects will be notified without undue delay if the breach is likely to result in a high risk to their rights and freedoms.
4. A breach register will be maintained documenting all incidents, even those not requiring notification.

### 18.9 Children's Data

As the hackathon targets participants aged 18 and above, the collection of data from minors is not anticipated. If a participant is found to be under 18, parental consent will be obtained immediately, or the participant will be excluded from the event.

- The Riara University venue will be assessed for wheelchair accessibility before the event.
- Accessible restrooms and ramps will be available at the event location.
